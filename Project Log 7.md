# Project Log 7 — Team Matrix and Competency Mapping

## 1. Core Capabilities and Diversity Matrix

Our founding team is primarily homogeneous, because all 4 members come from computer science or closely related technical tracks at the same university and share similar coursework, tools, and project experience. This overlap is a strength for early development velocity: we use the same languages and frameworks, communicate in the same technical vocabulary, and can review each other's code and divide engineering work with very little onboarding friction. The tradeoff is limited diversity of perspective, since none of us has professional experience in marketing, legal compliance, or business development, which makes us more likely to share the same blind spots when evaluating go-to-market decisions and user safety risks. Our differences are mostly in specialization, such as frontend, backend, and data or matching logic, and we will treat those as our internal diversity while deliberately seeking outside perspectives for everything else.

## 2. Skills Gap and Recruitment Pipeline

Our most significant gaps are trust and safety expertise, business development and partnership sales, and experience scaling cloud infrastructure and recommendation systems beyond a small user base. Trust and safety is especially critical because a platform that connects strangers in person carries moderation, verification, and privacy obligations that no one on our team has handled professionally. Over the next 12 months, we will first fill the partnerships gap by recruiting a campus partnerships and growth lead in the first six months, offering a small equity grant from our option pool that vests over four years with a one-year cliff, rather than cash salary we cannot yet afford. Between months six and twelve, we will prioritize a part-time trust and safety advisor or contractor and, once we have early funding, a senior engineer with experience in scalable backend or machine learning systems. We will screen for these hires through university entrepreneurship networks, LinkedIn outreach, and referrals from advisors, and we will use a short paid trial project before offering equity to reduce the risk of a mismatched hire.

## 3. De-Risking Network Strategy

To overcome our lack of institutional track record, we will build external validation through advisors, pilot partners, and structured startup programs rather than relying on our own credibility alone. We plan to recruit a small advisory group that includes a startup attorney or founder-experienced mentor for legal and corporate guidance, a trust and safety or online community moderation professional, and a faculty member or campus administrator who can vouch for us with the university. We will apply to the university's entrepreneurship center or incubator, regional student startup programs, and campus pitch competitions, since acceptance signals credibility to early users, event organizers, and prospective investors. We will also secure pilot partnerships with two or three student organizations at one campus and publicly document their results, because measurable outcomes such as event attendance and repeat matches serve as concrete proof that the product works. Together, these steps give buyers and investors third-party evidence that the venture is trustworthy, even though the company itself is new.
