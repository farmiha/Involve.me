# Project Log 6 — Legal Entity, Founders' Equity, and IP Protection

## 1. Entity Selection and Governance Framework

Involve.me will register as a Delaware C-Corporation, because our long-term capitalization strategy depends on raising outside capital, and most venture capital funds and angel investors expect to invest in C-Corps. A C-Corp allows multiple stock classes, such as common stock for founders and preferred stock for investors, and it supports a standard employee option pool, which an LLC handles awkwardly. For a software venture, this structure also protects the founders' personal assets through limited liability, which matters because we will handle user data and facilitate in-person meetings between strangers. Governance will be simple at first: the 4 founders will serve as the initial board of directors, decisions will pass by majority vote, and major actions such as issuing new equity, selling the company, or changing the business model will require approval from at least 3 of the 4 founders. We accept the double-taxation drawback of a C-Corp because we do not expect early profits, and the fundraising compatibility outweighs that cost.

## 2. Founders' Equity and Vesting Schedule

Our Founders' Agreement will split equity equally among the 4 founders at 25% each of the founder shares, because all members contribute at roughly the same level across engineering, product, and outreach. We will reserve an additional 10% option pool, carved out before any outside investment, to attract early hires and advisors without diluting the founders unexpectedly. Each founder's shares will vest over four years with a one-year cliff: no shares vest if a founder leaves in the first 12 months, 25% vests at the cliff, and the remainder vests monthly over the following 36 months. If a founder departs, the company will hold a repurchase right on all unvested shares at the original purchase price, so the equity of a team member who stops contributing returns to the company instead of remaining with someone who is no longer building it. Each founder will also file an 83(b) election with the IRS within 30 days of receiving their shares to avoid unexpected tax liability as the shares vest.

## 3. Software IP Protection Protocol

Our source code is automatically protected by copyright as soon as it is written, and we will register the copyright for key releases so we can pursue statutory damages if the code is copied. Because our repository is public, we will attach an explicit license file that states what others may and may not do with the code, rather than leaving usage rights ambiguous, and we will keep the matching logic and any proprietary data pipelines in private repositories. We will file a trademark application with the USPTO for the "Involve.me" name and logo after a clearance search, and we will secure matching domain and social handles so our brand cannot be claimed by someone else. Every founder, contractor, and outside developer will sign an IP Assignment Agreement transferring all work related to Involve.me to the corporation, which ensures the company, not an individual, owns the code, and we will also confirm that no founder's work is claimed by a university under its student IP policy. Before sharing non-public details with prospective partners, campus organizations, or event organizers, we will require a Non-Disclosure Agreement, and we will audit third-party open-source dependencies so their licenses do not conflict with our commercial model.
