**The Market Segmentation Matrix: Identify at least three distinct variables (e.g., demographic, behavioral, or technical characteristics) that you can use to segment your broad industry. Describe how you sliced your potential market into at least four distinct customer segments based on these variables.**

We segmented the broad industry using three variables. The first would be the life stage of the user, for example new movers, college students, college graduates, or young adults entering a new stage in their life in general. The second variable would be their social intent, which includes friendships, networking, nightlife, or dating.  The third is participation type, which is whether they are looking to go solo, as a couple, small group, or large group. From these variables, we produced four distinct customer segments: individuals who recently moved to a new city, college students seeking friends for events or nightlife, existing friend groups or couples looking for another group or pair to socialize with, and event-goers or established crews looking to add new members to their group. This is our initial plan that we will layer by adding event categories like concerts, sports, food, culture, etc. 


**The Target Niche Validation: Declare the specific target market segment your team has chosen to focus on. Provide an explicit, data-driven rationale explaining why this narrower niche represents the most viable entry point for your venture, focusing on factors like intensity of customer pain, accessibility, and lack of direct incumbent entrenchment.**

Our specific target market is young adults, roughly 18-29, building a social life in NYC, entering through verified college communities and a pilot with two or three student organizations at one campus. According to the American Psychiatric Association, 1 in 3 all adults experience loneliness weekly or regularly while the National Institute of Health reports that 50% of young adults within that age range feel lonely. Campus communities are also accessible through student organizations and events, and starting there builds our connection to the target market. We do not claim the space is free of incumbents, since Meetup, Bumble, Timeleft, and Partiful overlap with us, but our plan is to make more plan-specific crew matching that the rest of the market does not currently have.


**The Product Attribute Position: Define the two critical product attributes (e.g., cost vs. customization, speed vs. data security) that matter most to your target niche. Explain where your venture sits in relation to your primary rivals on a conceptual product attribute map, and write a concise, memorable 1-sentence tagline that communicates this unique market differentiation.**

The two critical product attributes that matter most to our target niche are the size of the social unit which is an individual versus a group, and the starting point of the match, which is the person’s profile versus a specific plan. Eventbrite and Partiful are more on the plan-centered side but those apps assume you already have people to bring. Bumble BFF and dating apps are person-centered and Meetup falls somewhere in between because it is organized around interest communities rather than a specific plan. Involve.me combines both concepts, where crews are matched with other crews or with newcomers for a specific event. Our tagline is “Know where to go, and know who to go with”
