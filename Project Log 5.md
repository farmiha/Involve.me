## Project Log 5 — Business Models for Software

### 1. Architecture Classification

Involve.me will use a standard freemium business model in which the core social experience remains free while optional premium features generate recurring revenue. Free access is important because the usefulness of a friendship and group-matching platform depends heavily on network size, so charging users before they can meaningfully participate would make the cold-start problem even harder. The free tier will include profile creation, basic individual and group matching, messaging, and event discovery, while paid tiers will offer features such as advanced matching filters, increased profile visibility, additional boosts, and expanded customization. This model aligns with our initial target of college students and young adults because it minimizes the cost of joining while allowing highly engaged users to pay for tools that improve discovery and matching.

### 2. Core Revenue and Monetization Strategy

Our initial pricing structure will include a Free tier at $0, an Involve+ tier at approximately $4.99 per month, and a Premium tier at approximately $9.99 per month. Rather than charging per message or match, our primary value unit will be the monthly user subscription: Involve+ will provide advanced preference filters and additional visibility tools, while Premium will include the highest level of matching customization, recurring profile boosts, and enhanced group and event discovery features. A secondary revenue stream could eventually allow event organizers or local organizations to pay a fixed fee to promote an event to relevant nearby users, without requiring ordinary users to pay to attend or discover events. We will monitor monthly paid-user churn, free-to-paid conversion, 30/60/90-day cohort retention, and cancellations by pricing tier; unusually high churn or low repeat usage would signal that the premium features are not providing enough continuing value.

### 3. Strategic Resource Matrix

Our most important intellectual assets will be the matching and recommendation logic, the growing social graph connecting users, groups, interests, and events, and the behavioral dataset generated as users interact with recommendations over time. Trust and safety systems, verification rules, accumulated preference data, and knowledge about which types of people, groups, and events produce successful connections could become increasingly difficult for a new competitor to reproduce even if the application code itself is copied. Our structural assets will include software engineering talent, reliable cloud infrastructure, authentication and notification services, moderation capabilities, and third-party integrations for services such as maps, event information, and identity or school verification. Partnerships with universities, student organizations, event organizers, and local communities will also be critical because they can supply both users and events, helping us create the local network density that makes the platform valuable.
