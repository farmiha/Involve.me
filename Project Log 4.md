## Project Log 4 — Industry Structure and Competitive Forces

### 1. Industry Trend Mapping

The social discovery industry is being shaped by growing demand for real-world connection among younger users, while existing platforms are increasingly expanding beyond one-to-one digital interaction toward groups and in-person events. Pew Research found that adults under 50 are substantially more likely than older adults to report frequent loneliness, while platforms such as Meetup and Bumble are actively investing in friendship, group discovery, and event-based features. Bumble has moved U.S. friendship users into a dedicated BFF application with group chats and events, and Meetup has recently invested in AI-powered recommendations and improved group and event discovery, showing that established companies see continued commercial value in this behavior. At the same time, privacy, location data, identity verification, and user safety are becoming increasingly important costs and trust requirements for companies that facilitate meetings between strangers, meaning the market has strong demand but also requires more sophisticated safety infrastructure than a typical social application.
### 2. Structural Force Assessment

The threat of substitute products is **high** because users do not need a dedicated application to meet new people: they can rely on existing friends, campus clubs, classes, group chats, Discord servers, social media, or simply attend events and meet people organically. These alternatives are usually free and already integrated into users' routines, which creates a very low switching cost and means Involve.me must provide substantially easier or better social discovery to change behavior. The threat of new entrants is also **high** because the basic technical components of our platform—profiles, matching algorithms, messaging, event listings, authentication, and cloud hosting—can be reproduced by another engineering team using widely available frameworks and managed services. Our public repository therefore cannot serve as the primary barrier to entry; the harder-to-copy advantage must eventually come from our user network, community relationships, behavioral data, and the quality of the matching experience.
### 3. Insulation Positioning

Our primary defense will be to build Involve.me around a localized network effect rather than relying on proprietary code alone. We plan to begin with verified college communities and combine individual matching, group matching, and event discovery into one workflow, so a user's existing friend group can discover another compatible group or event instead of starting every interaction with a one-to-one stranger match. As users participate, the platform can improve recommendations using information such as interests, group composition, event preferences, previous interactions, and optional attendance history, creating a dataset and social graph that a new competitor would not immediately possess. Over time, partnerships and integrations with campus organizations and event organizers could further strengthen this advantage by making Involve.me not simply another place to create a profile, but part of the workflow through which local communities discover people, form groups, and decide what to do together.
